# Moving to Shanghai to teach: an arrival checklist

Plan your move around confirmed dates, responsibilities and payments. Before you travel, know where you will stay, how you will reach it, who can help if plans change and which tasks need attention immediately after arrival.

An employer's offer of “arrival support” needs detail. Help finding an apartment is different from paying a deposit, and reimbursement is different from an expense paid directly.

## What should be confirmed before travel?

Ask the employer's recruitment contact to explain the application route and the conditions that must be met before travelling and starting work. Keep this separate from booking logistics: a preferred arrival date does not establish permission to work.

Use the [eligibility guide](teaching-eligibility-shanghai.md) to prepare your questions. Then write down the practical arrangements:

| Task | Confirmation to obtain |
| --- | --- |
| First accommodation | Exact address, check-in arrangements, length of stay and payer |
| Arrival transport | Airport or station, expected arrival time, route and backup |
| Phone access | A working way to contact your host and employer on arrival |
| Documents and appointments | Required originals, who arranges each appointment and timing |
| Early expenses | What you pay upfront and what may be reimbursed |
| First salary | Expected payment date and any first-month calculation |
| School contact | Named person and arrangements if you arrive outside their hours |

Save important addresses and contact details offline as well as on your phone.

## What needs attention when you arrive?

First, reach your accommodation and confirm that you can contact the people helping you. Make sure accommodation registration is handled promptly. Shanghai's official service page says foreign nationals staying outside hotels can use the national online system and must register within 24 hours of arriving at the accommodation. Follow the [current official instructions](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-ServicesandGuides-OnlineServices/20260922/665284ae21624250b916b1c0b570ff2f.html) for your situation.

For payments, consult the city's [bank-card and mobile-payment guidance](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Individuals-BankingandPayments-Payments/index.html). Test a small purchase and keep a backup method available; do not make your first essential transaction depend on an untested setup.

## How much money should you bring?

Build a dated list of your own expected payments rather than relying on a universal relocation figure. Include travel, temporary accommodation, deposit, advance rent, ordinary living costs before payday and a contingency.

Next to every reimbursement, record the expected payment date and conditions. If that date moves, decide how you would cover the gap. The [rental guide](renting-shanghai.md) explains why monthly rent alone is not an arrival budget.

## What can wait until you know the city better?

Avoid committing to a long commute before testing it. Visit possible homes, check the route at the times you will travel and ask what happens if your timetable changes. Use the [commute checklist](shanghai-metro-commute.md) before choosing a location.

Keep the first working weeks manageable: find your route, understand the school's routines and ask where to get help with unfamiliar tasks.

For a Flying English role, use [the job-page contact](https://flyingenglish-shanghai.com/jobs.html) to agree the school's actual support and your responsibilities. This checklist describes preparation, not a promise that the school pays every listed expense.
