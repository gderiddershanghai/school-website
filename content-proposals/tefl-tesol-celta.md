# TEFL, TESOL or CELTA: how to compare teaching courses

TEFL and TESOL are broad terms used in English-language teaching and course names. CELTA is a specific Cambridge qualification. Choose a course by its actual training, assessment and relevance to your intended job, not by the acronym alone.

TEFL means Teaching English as a Foreign Language; TESOL means Teaching English to Speakers of Other Languages. Course providers use the labels in overlapping ways, so the name does not tell you whether a course includes observed teaching practice. See [The TEFL Org's terminology guide](https://www.tefl.org/efl-teacher/efl-qualification/tefl-tesol-or-celta/) for the terminology, while evaluating its commercial course recommendations separately.

## What makes CELTA different?

Cambridge describes CELTA as a teaching qualification that includes practical teaching experience and observation. Its official page explains the course structure, assessment and available delivery formats. Use that [Cambridge course information](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/educators-organisations/professional-development/teaching-qualifications/celta/) when comparing a specific centre or course.

Do not assume that completing any named qualification automatically secures a job or work permission. Those are separate decisions.

## What should you compare?

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Will I teach real learners? | Reading about teaching and trying it are different experiences |
| Who observes me? | Useful feedback needs an identifiable, qualified tutor |
| What feedback will I receive? | Look for opportunities to revise and improve a lesson |
| What ages does the practice cover? | Teaching adults does not answer every young-learner classroom question |
| How is the course assessed? | Find out what you must demonstrate, not just how long you have access |
| What is the total cost? | Include materials, assessment and any travel or accommodation |
| Who awards or regulates it? | Check the specific claim with the named organization |

Ask for a sample timetable and assessment description. A headline number of course hours is only one part of the comparison.

## Is online or in-person training better?

Compare the actual experience. An online course may include scheduled live teaching and feedback, or it may be largely self-paced. An in-person course can also vary in the practice and support it provides.

Choose a format you can complete properly. Consider the time zone of live sessions, work commitments, deadlines and the equipment you need.

## Which course should you take for China?

Start with the requirements of the roles you are considering and verify recognition for the relevant application route. Avoid buying a course solely because an advertisement says it is “accepted everywhere.”

Read [Shanghai teaching eligibility](teaching-eligibility-shanghai.md), then compare the practical preparation you need. If you want to teach children, try the [young-learner lesson exercise](first-young-learner-lesson.md) and identify the skills you would want a tutor to help you develop.
