Do you need to speak Mandarin to teach English in China?

Flying English Center3 min readReviewed 20 October 2026

← All questions

Mandarin is not the same requirement in every teaching job. Check the language expectations of the particular role: delivering an English lesson, speaking with parents and joining staff meetings are different tasks. Outside school, learning useful Mandarin gives you more independence, even when you can get help in English.

How can you teach a language without translating everything?

You can make meaning visible through pictures, objects, demonstrations and examples. To explain “put the card on the table,” show the action first. Then let a learner demonstrate it before the group begins.

That does not make every classroom English-only. A school's approach to using learners' first language, and the support available from colleagues, should be clear. When comparing roles, ask who handles complex explanations and family communication.

Our first lesson plan shows how a simple activity can move from recognition to speaking without a long explanation.

Which Mandarin should you learn first?

Choose situations you repeat. A useful starting list is your address, numbers and prices, directions, ordering food, describing a problem at home and asking someone to repeat or slow down.

Keep the written Chinese for your home and workplace on your phone. Being able to show an accurate address can help before you can pronounce it reliably.

Situation A useful learning goal
Buying lunch Understand the price and ask for the item you want
Finding an address Recognize the building name and street
Receiving a delivery Explain where you are and understand a simple location question
Renting a home Recognize recurring words for rent, deposit and repairs
Asking for help Explain that you are learning and request a slower repetition

Shanghai's Learn Chinese resources provide a place to explore local language-learning material.

How do you build a habit around teaching hours?

Pick a small recurring practice slot and one real-world task. For example, learn a short food-ordering exchange, practise it with a tutor or language partner, and use it on your next visit. Review what you did not understand instead of immediately adding a long vocabulary list.

If your days off fall during the week, look for a class schedule that fits those days. Check attendance and cancellation terms before paying for a course.

Can translation apps do everything?

They can help you get started, but a translated sentence is not proof that a contract or important instruction has been understood. Use appropriate human help for consequential decisions. For everyday exchanges, check names, numbers and addresses carefully.
